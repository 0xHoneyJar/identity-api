Identity API Authentication & Linking Options

A portfolio decision for near-zero-friction wallet authentication and near-term Discord, Telegram, and wallet linking—without surrendering identity-api's platform- and chain-agnostic source-of-record role.

Decision: Build one provider-neutral credential broker and durable session in identity-api; adopt Telegram OIDC, Discord OAuth, and guarded bot-linking; make Reown SIWX/One-Click the preferred wallet route with SIWE fallback; add passkeys next; keep managed vendors behind reversible ports.

Why the model favors a portfolio, not one vendor

These options operate at different layers. OAuth/OIDC proves a Discord or Telegram subject; SIWX proves a chain account; Reown improves wallet transport; passkeys improve return authentication; managed wallet vendors can create keys and UX. None of those should decide canonical identity linkage by itself.

AHP criteria and weights

The pairwise judgments deliberately prioritize the user's stated near-term channels and the ratified architecture. Security, chain neutrality, and friction are co-equal load-bearing criteria. Maturity, implementation speed, sovereignty, and session continuity remain material; operational burden and commercial TCO are lower-weight because they are more reversible than an identity or security mistake.

What experts and standards largely agree on—and where they do not

Broad agreement

  • Use immutable provider issuer + subject identifiers, not mutable handles or email, as the link key.
  • Use authorization code, PKCE S256, exact redirect matching, issuer/context validation, and a transaction bound to the initiating session.
  • Persist a server-side session so ordinary returning visits do not require another wallet signature.
  • Treat wallet connection and wallet authentication as distinct proofs, even when a wallet combines their UI.
  • Use passkeys for phishing-resistant returning authentication and account recovery after identity establishment.
  • Keep chain account identifiers and signed proofs versioned and namespace-aware.

Real disagreement or uncertainty

  • Synced vs. device-bound passkeys: synced credentials improve recovery and AAL2 usability; device-bound credentials offer stronger isolation and high-assurance properties.
  • Managed identity vs. sovereign identity: vendors optimize conversion; the cost is dependency, migration complexity, and authority ambiguity.
  • One-click wallet standard: Reown is practical now, while CAIP-222, ERC-7846, and ERC-8019 have different scopes and uncertain wallet convergence.
  • Bot/deep-link linking: product teams value the convenience; security guidance emphasizes session fixation, phishing, forwarding, and cross-device confirmation risk.
  • Capabilities in login: ReCaps/identity keys are powerful, but may be premature complexity until a named consumer needs delegated actions.

Further questions that can change the decision

  1. What percentage of near-term users enter through Telegram, Discord, an existing external wallet, or no wallet at all?
  2. Which chains and account namespaces must be supported in the first two worlds beyond EVM?
  3. Are embedded wallets a platform capability, or a product/world-level choice?
  4. What assurance level is required for ordinary login, credential linking, recovery, treasury actions, and identity merges?
  5. Can one central identity origin serve all worlds, or must unrelated domains use a top-level authorization-code/PKCE exchange?
  6. What measured drop-off occurs at connect, sign, OAuth redirect, bot confirmation, and return-session resume?
  7. Which commercial vendors will contractually support export, incident evidence, regional requirements, and predictable cost at projected scale?

Caveats and assumptions

  • The option ratings are calibrated analyst judgments grounded in current primary documentation; they are not production conversion, latency, incident, or price measurements.
  • Vendor pricing, enterprise features, SLAs, and roadmap commitments require direct quotes and technical diligence.
  • Draft/Review wallet standards are scored for architectural promise but should remain behind stable internal interfaces and capability detection.
  • The matrix scores each option's contribution to the target. Complementary options can all be adopted even when their ordinal ranks differ.
  • The June 1 internal record described production as Dynamic-backed and identity-api as deployment-lagged; live infrastructure and current telemetry were not probed in this report.
